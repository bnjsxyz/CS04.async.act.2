    1 Level 1 · Notice

    Your body is giving you data.

    Physiological well-being means supporting the body’s systems so you can function, recover, learn, relate, and respond to daily demands. Awareness comes before action.

    Identify body signalsConnect habits to well-beingChoose a safe first response
    M

    Move

    Planned activity supports cardiovascular endurance, muscular strength, flexibility, balance, and confidence in daily tasks.

    B

    Breathe

    Mindfulness trains attention to return to the present calmly, which can support focus, emotional regulation, and self-awareness.

    R

    Recreate

    Enjoyable leisure offers refreshment, connection, and a sense of belonging. Recreation should restore—not drain—you.

    A balanced approach: Fitness challenges the body appropriately, mindfulness helps you notice and regulate, and recreation restores energy and connection. No single activity does every job.

    2 Level 2 · Activate

    Fitness is planned movement with a purpose.

    Fitness activities are structured, repetitive forms of physical activity used to improve or maintain fitness. The strongest routine is safe, enjoyable, and realistic enough to repeat.

    Classify four activity typesApply start-slow guidanceRecognize safety signals
    ♥

    Aerobic

    Sustained movement that makes the heart and lungs work harder.

    Examples: brisk walking, jogging, swimming, cycling, dancing.

    S

    Strengthening

    Resistance or body-weight work that builds and tones muscles.

    Examples: squats, lunges, push-ups, planks.

    ↔

    Flexibility

    Movement that supports an easier, wider range of motion.

    Examples: stretching, yoga, shoulder rolls.

    ◎

    Balance

    Practice that improves stability and body control.

    Examples: one-foot stance, heel-to-toe walk, tai chi.

    A

    Build a balanced starter set

    Tap at least three types. Notice that “balanced” can be simple and equipment-free.

    Select activities to preview your starter set.
    Safety compass: Warm up and cool down, drink water, build up gradually, and listen to your body. Stop if you feel dizzy, excessively tired, or in pain. Ask a trusted adult or qualified health professional when symptoms persist or you are unsure what is safe for you.
    3 Level 3 · Regulate

    Mindfulness is attention without harsh judgment.

    Mindfulness practices train you to notice the present moment calmly and return when attention wanders. The goal is not to “empty” the mind—it is to practice returning.

    Practice paced breathingUse the five sensesRespond gently to distraction

    What it can support

    • Calmer responses to stress and anxiety
    • Focus, memory, and emotional control
    • Gratitude, self-awareness, and stronger relationships

    Ways to practice

    • Mindful breathing and body scans
    • Five-senses noticing
    • Mindful walking, seeing, listening, posing, or movement

    6 Level 6 · Integrate

    Well-being is a pattern, not a perfect day.

    The goal is to notice, choose, try, and adjust. A supportive routine combines appropriate movement, present-moment regulation, enjoyable renewal, and enough recovery.

    Analyze a whole-day scenarioEvaluate a planAdjust without giving up

    The four-step loop

    1. Notice: What is my body and mind signaling?
    2. Choose: What kind of support fits the need?
    3. Try: Take one safe, realistic action.
    4. Adjust: Review the effect and refine the plan.

    Evidence from your life

    After a week, check patterns in energy, mood, focus, sleep, discomfort, and consistency. These observations help you make a smaller, safer, or better-timed plan.

    Setbacks are information. They do not erase progress.

    Scope and support: This learning activity supports education and self-reflection; it does not diagnose or treat health conditions. Persistent pain, dizziness, breathing difficulty, or major changes in physical or emotional well-being deserve help from a trusted adult and qualified professional.
